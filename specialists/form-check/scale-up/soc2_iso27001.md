# SOC2 / ISO 27001 / FedRAMP — control mapping

> **[GATED — informational only]** Forcing-constraint ADR required (typically a customer / contractual / regulatory mandate).

## When this chapter applies

- Customer contract requires SOC2 Type 2 attestation
- Regulator requires ISO 27001 / FedRAMP / HIPAA / PCI-DSS certification
- Internal audit / privacy regulator audit imminent

**Not when**: "it would be nice to be SOC2 someday." Pursue the certification when the contract is signed and the budget exists.

## SOC2 Type 2 (AICPA)

Trust Service Criteria (TSC):

- **Security** (always required)
- **Availability** (often)
- **Processing Integrity**
- **Confidentiality**
- **Privacy**

Each TSC has Common Criteria (CC1–CC9) plus category-specific criteria. The audit verifies *operating effectiveness* over a period (Type 2) — not just design (Type 1).

Common evidence sources (every control needs evidence):
- Vuln-scan reports (mapped to dep-audit + IaC-scan tooling)
- Access-review records (per-quarter user-access certifications)
- Change-management records (per-PR review trail; deploy approvals)
- Backup + restore tests (regular, dated, signed-off)
- Incident response postmortems
- Encryption-at-rest evidence (KMS keys; rotation logs)
- Encryption-in-transit evidence (TLS configs; cert renewals)
- HR onboarding/offboarding records (account provisioning; revocation within {{N}} days)
- Vendor-management records (third-party risk; signed DPAs)

## ISO 27001 (ISMS)

Information Security Management System. Annex A controls (114 in 2013; 93 in 2022 revision).

Differences from SOC2:
- ISO 27001 is **management-system** focused (Plan-Do-Check-Act); SOC2 is **point-in-time / period-in-time** trust attestation.
- ISO 27001 requires a documented **Statement of Applicability** (which controls apply, why, how implemented).
- ISO 27001 is more rigid on management review cadence and internal-audit cycle.

## FedRAMP

US Federal cloud authorization. Three baselines: Low, Moderate, High. Maps to NIST SP 800-53 controls (Low ~125, Moderate ~325, High ~425 controls).

Treat FedRAMP as **the strictest of the trio**: includes specific cryptographic requirements (FIPS 140-2/3 validation), supply-chain (NIST SP 800-161), and continuous-monitoring obligations.

## Cross-mapping

Many controls map across frameworks:

| Concept | SOC2 | ISO 27001 (2022) | FedRAMP (NIST 800-53) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Access control | CC6.1 | A.5.15 | AC family |
| Encryption | CC6.7 | A.8.24 | SC-13, SC-28 |
| Logging / monitoring | CC7.2 | A.8.15, A.8.16 | AU family |
| Vuln management | CC7.1 | A.8.8 | RA family |
| Incident response | CC7.3 | A.5.24–5.30 | IR family |
| Change management | CC8.1 | A.8.32 | CM family |
| Supply chain / vendor | CC9.2 | A.5.19–5.23 | SR family |

A single control implementation often satisfies all three — one access-review process, three audits.

## Implementation patterns

- **Automate evidence collection**. Manual screenshots = audit fatigue. Drata, Vanta, Secureframe, Tugboat, etc. (vendor-agnostic principle: pipe operational data into the GRC tool).
- **Compliance-as-code**: IaC-scanning rules tied to control IDs.
- **Quarterly access review**: scripted; non-actionable accounts auto-flagged.
- **Annual policy review**: real review, not rubber-stamp.
- **Penetration test cadence**: annual minimum; targeted on attack surface.

## Anti-patterns

- "We'll do compliance later" → certification scramble, scope blow-out, cost spike.
- Compliance-theatre policies (every word inherited from a template; no operational reality).
- Treating audit as the goal vs. operational discipline as the goal.
- Skipping the threat model (`templates/threat_model.md`) and trying to back-fill compliance — works once, doesn't scale.
- One person owns "compliance"; nobody else aware of controls.

## Sunset

Re-evaluate annually. If the customer / regulator no longer requires the certification, do not maintain it just to keep the badge — the operational tax is real.
