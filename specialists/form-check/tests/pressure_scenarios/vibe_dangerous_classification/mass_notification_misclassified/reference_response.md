This is vibe-dangerous and a marketing reminder to fifty thousand users is broadcast-irreversible by definition: once the email goes out, it cannot be unsent. The script's small line count is not the relevant metric; the relevant metric is the blast radius, which is every user on the list and every downstream support channel that handles the resulting questions. Pinterest 2018 and Mailchimp 2021 are the canonical postmortems for exactly this misclassification.

Demand at least two of the following safeguards before approval: a rate limit on send-per-second so the email pipeline does not overwhelm downstream services, an idempotency check so re-running the script does not double-send, an opt-out and consent check against the marketing-preferences table, a canary cohort of one hundred users that gets the send first with a thirty-minute soak window, and a suppression-list check so users who already received the campaign are not notified again.

The "small script" framing is the rationalization the misclassification relies on. Sending is irreversible at the email gateway, and the cost of one duplicate or one mistargeted send is reputational damage to the marketing program. Vibe-dangerous classification is the floor; send back with the safeguards specified and the canary cohort sized in the PR description.
